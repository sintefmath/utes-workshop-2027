Storing heat underground – in boreholes (BTES), aquifers (ATES) or fractured rock (FTES) – is a scalable way to move surplus and renewable heat from summer to winter. Such underground thermal energy storage (UTES) is poised to play a larger role in future energy systems, with full-scale plants now being built and run in Norway and elsewhere.

Every storage system is shaped by its subsurface, which is complex and only partly known. Groundwater flow, fractures and heterogeneous rock control how much heat can be recovered, but direct data is incomplete and hard to obtain. Decisions about design, monitoring and day-to-day operation therefore involve considerable uncertainty. Modelling and simulation can reduce that uncertainty, especially when models are continuously updated with operational data, but only if the tools fit the practical questions people actually face.

This workshop brings together those who develop models and those who plan, build and operate real storage systems, to share experience, compare approaches and discuss where modelling and digital twins make a real difference today.

What the workshop is about

  • BTES and FTES/ATES: modelling for design, performance assessment and operation
  • Heat losses and groundwater interaction: understanding, estimating and reducing them
  • Fractured and heterogeneous subsurface: when does geological complexity matter?
  • Learning from data: monitoring, calibration and continuously updated models
  • Optimising design and operation under uncertainty and practical constraints
  • Digital twins in practice: what is useful today, and what is generic versus site-specific

Format

In the workshop, we aim to include:

  • keynote talks from invited experts
  • short presentations and "pitches" from industry and research
  • demonstrations of modelling tools and case studies
  • moderated discussions on shared challenges and priorities

We aim to finish with a shared view of the most important open questions, and ideas for follow-up collaboration.

Who should attend

  • Researchers and students working on geothermal energy, subsurface flow or energy systems
  • Owners, operators and developers of geothermal and thermal storage systems
  • District heating companies, consultants, drilling and engineering companies
  • Public bodies and anyone else interested in the role of geological heat storage in the energy transition

Background

The workshop is organised by SINTEF Digital. It builds on the research project GHOST DigiT, which develops simulation and digital twin technology for geological heat storage with industry partners and case studies in Tromsø, Asker, Drammen and Narvik. GHOST DigiT is funded by the Research Council of Norway (project 344540) and its industry partners.
